title: Declared errors
description: Model expected domain failures, map them to stable HTTP contracts, and decide whether asynchronous work may retry.
---

A declared error is an expected outcome a caller can understand and handle. Examples include an unknown
order, insufficient inventory, or a temporary upstream rejection. Unexpected exceptions and programmer
defects are not declared errors; they remain internal and are redacted at public boundaries.

The `id` is the durable contract used in graphs, clients, traces, and error matching. `data` contains the
bounded details safe for callers. `message` may derive a readable explanation. `http.status` contributes a
typed route response, and `retry` tells durable delivery whether another attempt may help.

## Choose retry policy

<Tabs items={["Never", "Later", "Delayed"]}>
  <Tab value="Never">Use `retry: "never"` when identical input cannot succeed, such as a permanently invalid state.</Tab>
  <Tab value="Later">Use `retry: "later"` for a transient condition when the provider's normal backoff is sufficient.</Tab>
  <Tab value="Delayed">Use `{ kind: "later", afterMs }` to provide a minimum delay. The durable provider may wait longer.</Tab>
</Tabs>

HTTP and direct function invocation do not automatically retry. Jobs and event listeners combine declared
retry metadata with their own bounded retry policy. A retryable declaration is therefore guidance, not an
unbounded loop.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not declare arbitrary exceptions">
  Converting every caught exception into a public error leaks implementation detail and makes
  callers depend on unstable failures. Declare only outcomes the domain promises to handle.
</Callout>
